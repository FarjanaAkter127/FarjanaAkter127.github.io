About

I am a PhD candidate in Applied Mathematics at the University of Toledo, working under the supervision of Dr. Chunhua Shan. My dissertation research centers on high-codimension bifurcation theory and its applications in mathematical biology, with a focus on predator-prey systems and epidemic dynamics.

My work analyzes how the topological structure of vector fields changes near degenerate equilibria, uncovering limit cycles and global bifurcation diagrams in biologically motivated systems where standard low-codimension tools are insufficient. I work with stability analysis, normal form computation, and numerical simulation (MATLAB, Mathematica, Maple, MatCont, and Python).

I also serve as a Research Assistant with Dr. William Kalies, where I apply machine learning and data-driven methods to the study of dynamical systems.
